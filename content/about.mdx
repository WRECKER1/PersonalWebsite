I came to finance sideways. My degree is in Computer Science and Engineering from UCLA, and for a while I assumed I would spend my working life building software. What changed my mind was running a business of my own.

## From building to allocating

When you operate a company, the questions that keep you awake are rarely technical. They are questions about capital: where the next rupee should go, how long cash will last, whether growth is worth what it costs. I found I enjoyed those questions more than any others, and that I wanted to understand how the best investors and managers answer them.

## The CFA Program

I began the CFA Program to give that curiosity a structure. I am now a Level III candidate. The curriculum has been less about memorising formulas than about building a common language — for reading financial statements, for valuing a business, and for thinking about risk honestly.

## What interests me

I am most interested in the work of understanding a business well enough to have an opinion about its value: how it earns its returns, whether those returns can last, and what management does with the cash. That interest points towards investment research, asset management and wealth management, and more broadly towards anything that involves allocating capital carefully.

## Why keep a journal

Because writing exposes weak thinking faster than anything else I know. Publishing a thesis with a date on it means I cannot later pretend I believed something else. I expect to be wrong often here. The [retrospectives](/retrospectives) exist so that being wrong is at least useful.
