## Executive summary

A bank is a spread business. Small finance banks earn high yields because they lend to borrowers larger banks avoid, and most analysis focuses there. This note argues the more durable advantage sits on the other side of the balance sheet: the cost and stability of deposits.

## Investment thesis

Among lenders with similar loan books, the one that funds itself most cheaply can price loans lower, pick better borrowers, and still earn the same spread. Over a full credit cycle, that selection effect matters more than headline yield.

## Industry overview

Small finance banks typically began as microfinance or vehicle-finance companies and converted to banks to gain access to deposits. The conversion solved a funding problem but created a new one: building a retail deposit base from nothing, in competition with banks that have had one for decades.

## Business model analysis

The useful questions are about granularity and behaviour. What share of deposits are small retail balances? How much comes from bulk depositors who will leave for a slightly better rate? How much is the bank paying above the market to attract money?

A bank paying a premium rate for deposits has not built a franchise. It has rented one.

## Financial analysis

Four measures do most of the work when comparing banks on this dimension:

| Measure | What it tells you | What to watch for |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Cost of funds | The raw price of money | A gap to peers that is not narrowing |
| Low-cost deposit share | Quality of the franchise | Growth driven only by rate offers |
| Top-20 depositor concentration | Stability | Reliance on a few bulk depositors |
| Credit–deposit ratio | How stretched funding is | Loans growing much faster than deposits |

## Valuation

Price-to-book is the conventional lens, but it only makes sense next to sustainable return on equity. A bank with a real deposit franchise deserves a premium because its returns are less dependent on taking credit risk to earn them.

## Risks and catalysts

- **Risk:** a credit cycle in unsecured lending that overwhelms any funding advantage.
- **Risk:** deposit competition from larger banks raising rates.
- **Catalyst:** a transition to a universal banking licence, which may lower funding costs further.

## Conclusion

When studying any lender in this group, start with the liabilities. The asset side tells you how much a bank earns this year. The liability side tells you whether it will still be earning it in ten.
