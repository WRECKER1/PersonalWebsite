## Executive summary

Halden Industries has announced the demerger of its consumer appliances division into a separately listed company. Shareholders will receive one share in the new entity for each share held. This note looks at the situation through the usual special-situation questions: why is it happening, who will be forced to sell, and what is each piece worth alone?

## Investment thesis

Combined, the two businesses are valued on a blended multiple that suits neither. Separation allows each to be valued against its real peers. The opportunity, if there is one, lies in the period after listing when index funds and mandate-constrained holders sell the smaller entity regardless of price.

## Industry overview

The engineering business is cyclical, capital-heavy and tied to industrial investment. The consumer business is steadier, asset-light and brand-driven. Investors who want one rarely want the other.

## Business model analysis

| | Engineering (parent) | Consumer (spin-off) |
| :-- | --: | --: |
| Share of revenue | 68% | 32% |
| Share of operating profit | 51% | 49% |
| Capital employed | 81% | 19% |
| Return on capital employed | 11% | 38% |

The consumer division earns nearly half the profit on a fifth of the capital. Inside the conglomerate, that is hard to see.

## Financial analysis

The key detail is the allocation of debt and corporate costs. In the scheme document used for this sample, all borrowings stay with the parent. The spin-off begins life debt-free, which flatters it and burdens what remains.

## Valuation

A sum-of-the-parts approach, valuing each business on a conservative peer multiple, suggests a combined value around 20% above the pre-announcement price. Most of that gap comes from re-rating the consumer business.

## Risks and catalysts

- **Risk:** the parent is left with too much debt for a cyclical business.
- **Risk:** the re-rating happens before listing, leaving nothing on the table.
- **Catalyst:** listing of the consumer entity and the forced selling that follows.

## Conclusion

The structure is sound and the logic is clear. Whether there is an opportunity depends entirely on the price in the first weeks after listing. See the [notebook entry](/ideas/halden-industries) for how this one played out.
