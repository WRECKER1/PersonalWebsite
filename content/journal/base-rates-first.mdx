Every investment case is a story about a specific company. The story is usually persuasive, because someone has worked to make it so. The trouble is that persuasive stories are available for the companies that go on to disappoint as well.

The corrective is to step outside the story and ask a duller question first: of all the companies that looked like this one, how many achieved what this one is being assumed to achieve?

## Inside view, outside view

The inside view builds a forecast from the details of the case. The outside view ignores the details and looks at the reference class. Most forecasts lean heavily on the first and neglect the second.

A sensible process starts with the outside view and adjusts for specifics, in that order. Starting with the specifics and then checking the base rate rarely works, because by then the conclusion has formed.

## In practice

When a model shows a company sustaining high growth, high margins and high returns for many years, I try to check each assumption against how often it has actually happened. Sustained excellence is rarer than spreadsheets make it look.

This does not mean never betting on an exceptional business. It means knowing that I am betting on an exception, and asking for evidence in proportion.
