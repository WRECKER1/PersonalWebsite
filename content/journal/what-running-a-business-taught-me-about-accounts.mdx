Before I ran a business, financial statements were documents to analyse. Afterwards, they were the residue of decisions I recognised.

## Revenue is a decision about timing

From the outside, revenue is a fact. From the inside, there is nearly always judgement about when a sale counts. That does not make it dishonest. It does mean the cash flow statement deserves equal attention.

## Working capital is where growth is paid for

The first time sales grew quickly, cash got tighter, not looser. Customers paid later than suppliers needed paying. No textbook description of the cash conversion cycle landed the way that month did.

## Every cost line has a person behind it

Cutting a cost on a spreadsheet takes a keystroke. In an operating business it is a conversation. I read management commentary about efficiency programmes with more patience now, and more scepticism about timelines.
