Levels I and II are largely about the asset: what it is, how it is accounted for, what it is worth. Level III turns to the investor. The same bond is suitable for one portfolio and wrong for another, and the curriculum spends its time on why.

## What feels different

The questions are constructed responses, not only multiple choice. You have to write an answer and justify it. That is closer to the real work than anything in the earlier exams, and harder to prepare for by recognition alone.

## Ideas I want to keep

- An investment policy statement is a discipline for the adviser as much as the client.
- Risk is defined by the liability, not the asset. A pension fund and an endowment can hold the same security and face different risks.
- Behavioural biases are not a side topic. They are treated as inputs to portfolio construction.
